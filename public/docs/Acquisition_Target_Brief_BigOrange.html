Cincinnati Metro · est. 2017 · ~5 people · WBENC women-owned · asking $790K · full or majority

The honest read, up front

Best-fit target you could plausibly find — wrapped around a margin that's halving and a price built on a projection. On strategy this is almost too on-thesis: B2B service-company clients (MSP/IT, home builders, landscaping, pro services) that outsource marketing entirely — that's Marin's buyer, a clean Trades/Swell adjacency, in Ohio, women-owned, with a seller open to employee-ownership structures.

On substance, two things gate it. SDE has fallen ~50% in two years on flat revenue, and the ask is priced off a 2026 recovery the seller hasn't shown you how they'll hit. And "5 employees" is three founders and ~two staff — the value is those three women's relationships and certifications, which is exactly what your guide says can walk out the door. Keep talking. Get the data. Don't price off their projection.

01Does it belong in the family?

Your first gate, before valuation. Two clear passes, one open question.

  • Flywheel pass Their clients are Trades Track buyers almost exactly — MSP/IT, residential builders/developers, professional services. Their book becomes Wonderly/Swell pipeline; Swell's engine could feed theirs. This genuinely feeds what you run.
  • Which branch clear Trades adjacency / Swell demand engine — not a new branch, not "cheap and available." A productized, niched, outsourced-marketing shop is on-map.
  • Operator unresolved — the whole deal hinges here Owner "willing to stay 6mo–3yr" is a transition runway, not a managing partner. You need to know which founder(s) stay and whether one is a true managing-partner candidate. No operator who owns it → you'd be buying a job, which is the one thing you're leaving.

03The one question that decides it

Everything else is diligence. This is the fork.

If it's this → opportunity

Fixable ops problem

Margin fell because they added the office, over-hired, or founders drew up. Your pricing, utilization, and shared-services discipline restore it. You buy at trailing price and fix the exact thing you're good at fixing. This is extend a strength.

If it's this → walk

Structural compression

MSP-marketing is crowded and commoditizing — they publish head-to-head "vs. competitor" pages, and everyone's bolting "AI-driven" onto the same StoryBrand playbook. If pricing power is eroding market-wide, you're catching a falling knife.

04What to ask in this room

Tailored from your guide to what this specific business is hiding.

Ask the founders, directly
  • Why sell now — really? (Then check it against a book that halved its SDE.)
  • Walk me through the SDE decline. What changed from 2023 to today?
  • The 2026 jump to $215K — what specifically gets you there on flat revenue?
  • Of the five, who are the three founders — and which of you stays, how long, doing what? What walks with whoever leaves?
  • What share of revenue is contract retainer with minimum terms vs. project vs. hourly?
  • Top three clients — what percent of revenue, and are they under contract or loyal to one of you?
  • Does the WBENC certification survive a change of ownership? On what conditions?
  • Three years of tax returns and bank statements, not just the P&L — and would you carry a seller note and take an earnout?

05If it became a deal — the shape that protects you

  • Anchor on trailing, not projected. Open near trailing-SDE value (~$400–500K zone), not their $790K. Their number lives in the upside, not the base.
  • Make the projection an earnout. It's their 2026 forecast — so let them carry it. Price climbs toward their number only if the SDE recovery actually lands. Straight out of your key-person playbook.
  • Transition + non-compete on all three founders. With value this concentrated in three people, a handoff and a real non-compete aren't optional — a departing founder rebuilding this across the street is the risk.
  • Whose balance sheet, decided on purpose. Under The Wonder Jam → Allie's guarantee, and the WBENC-preservation question runs through her ownership. Personal deal → your guarantee. Pick the fork deliberately.
  • New entity, don't dilute the anchor. Its own LLC / QSub; keep any partner equity in the branch, never in the certified anchor.

06Your own timing gate

Where this sits against what you've said has to come first.

  • Your guide gates acquisition on an operator and a shared-services spine — the spine isn't built yet.
  • Your current binding constraint is financial instrumentation you haven't finished. Buying someone else's messy books before your own are clean compounds the problem.
  • It doesn't fix your margin — it imports a thinner, declining one. Unit economics before demand.
  • The exception that would change the timing: if one founder is a genuine managing-partner operator who stays and owns it, this could accelerate the spine instead of straining it. That's the thing worth finding out.
