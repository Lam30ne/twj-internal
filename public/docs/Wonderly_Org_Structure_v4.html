One House, Two Teams

The team organized around the two outcomes it owns — revenue & satisfaction on one side, efficiency & quality on the other. Camila's v4.0 chart, adopted and pressure-tested.

Version · v4.0 Date · July 2026 Author · Camila Camargo Audience · Full Leadership Team

What this replaces. On the org-structure dimension, this supersedes the Front-of-House / Back-of-House model in the Operating Framework and Strategic Operating Brief. Those documents remain the source of truth for everything else — scorecards, financial targets, brief frameworks, the two-track go-to-market. The crosswalk below maps the old role names to the new ones so nothing gets lost in the rename.

Wonderly is the biggest fan of its clients — and believes not just in creating the highest-quality value, but in delivering it in the best way possible. We help personality-driven brands turn their momentum into something they actually own.

With that in mind, we organize the team around two groups defined by the outcome each one owns: a Clients Team and a Production Team. The split separates people by what they are accountable for — revenue and satisfaction on one side, efficiency and quality on the other. The Clients Team is judged on how much revenue the client relationship generates (new bookings, upsells, renewals) and how satisfied the client stays throughout. The Production Team is judged on efficiency (utilization, budget and schedule variance, cycle time) and quality (craft standards, revision rates, Quality Gate pass rate).

Note on axes: "Clients / Production" is the internal accountability structure. "Talent / Trades" remains the client-type axis — it now lives inside the Client Consultant role as the three archetypes (Allie · Adam · Matt), not as a separate org chart. The two axes are complementary, not competing.

Clients Team

Goal: Revenue & Satisfaction

Tier-One · Adam · Allie · Matt

Client Consultant

Carries forward the core value of deep listening on the inquiry, qualifying fit, and framing the strategic shape of the engagement by archetype — Allie for Talent, Adam for brand-forward Trade, Matt for technically complex builds. The Consultant is the domain expert Wonderly leans on to diagnose what a client actually needs.

With the Client Manager now owning sales and closing, the Consultant's role narrows toward the front-end diagnostic and strategic framing — re-entering at key moments (kickoff and renewal) rather than owning the deal through to close. Consultants also attend the quarterly strategy meeting for premium-tier clients.

Three peers, each anchored to a natural client archetype, operating at the same altitude with clients rather than in a hierarchy. Matt's domain is both narrow and broad: he leads Trade clients where technical complexity is the primary reason they hired Wonderly, and any client — Trade or Talent — where technical leadership is what the engagement fundamentally requires. If the ask is "build us a sophisticated platform," Matt is the Consultant, regardless of track. Many clients already perceive Adam, Allie, and Matt this way; the framing simply makes explicit what is effectively true.

Altitude — enters at the front-end diagnostic and at renewal; the strategist, not the closer.

⚑ Observation (Camila)

This is the piece of the restructure most worth pressure-testing before rollout.

Primary Revenue Owner

Client Manager

Owns the client relationship from handoff through every renewal — holding judgment calls, leading the closing and membership conversations, and now owning sales as well. The Client Manager is the primary revenue owner inside the Clients Team: qualifying and closing new business, and owning upsell and renewal for the life of the account. This consolidation is what lets Wonderly point to one person per account accountable for revenue from first conversation to renewal.

They approve every client-facing communication, lead every significant conversation, and step in whenever a situation requires judgment rather than execution. They are not running logistics or chasing confirmations — the Coordinator owns that. Their job is to be the person the client trusts most, present in exactly the right moments to deepen that trust.

Altitude — hold altitude, present for decisions.

Satisfaction Backbone

Client Coordinator

The operational backbone of the client experience — systems setup, scheduling, day-to-day communication, service-request triage, and proactive flagging of client sentiment. This is the role most directly responsible for the satisfaction half of the team's goal: response time, smooth onboarding, and nothing falling through the cracks.

The principle: no client should ever feel the coordination behind their experience. That feeling is engineered — the result of three roles operating in their lanes, handing off cleanly, and trusting each other to do their part without being chased.

Altitude — the ground game; owns the day-to-day so the Manager can hold altitude.

⚑ Open Item (Camila) — the sales handoff

Moving sales ownership from Client Consultant to Client Manager changes the Fit Call → How Call → Close sequence the old process was built around. Before rollout, decide: does the Consultant still join the Fit Call as the domain expert, or does the Manager run point from first contact with the Consultant looped in only for complex/technical scoping?

Production Team

Goal: Efficiency & Quality

Execution + Economics

Project Manager

Owns both the execution of individual projects and the financial and operational stewardship of Production as a whole. On individual projects: schedules, task assignment, cross-team coordination between Design, Development, and Marketing, and quality checkpoints before work ships. Leads the internal kickoff, sets the quality benchmark, and briefs the production team on who the client is and what excellent looks like — carrying the Clients Team's strategic framing through into the brief.

Across Production as a whole: owns unit economics, capacity planning, resource allocation, hiring, tooling, and margin discipline. A peer to the craft and platform leads — with veto authority on cost, and no authority on craft. They don't make the work better; the craft leads do that. They make sure the conditions exist for the work to be better: right people on right projects, honest capacity, visible costs, protected margin — so nobody (including Allie) has to context-switch from craft to spreadsheets to keep the business healthy.

Runs the operational cadence: weekly 1:1s with each craft lead, weekly margin dashboard refresh, monthly margin review with Allie, quarterly Production retro.

Altitude — one internal presence at every kickoff and quality checkpoint; the steward of Production's health.

⚑ Observation (Camila)

This merges two full-time mandates — hands-on project execution and Production-wide economics/capacity — into one seat. Worth watching as project volume grows: the split may need to come back once one person can no longer hold both well.

Design Team — organized by seniority

Instead of a single lead directing an undifferentiated pool, Design is tiered: Senior designers take flagship and high-complexity work and mentor; Mid-level designers execute independently on standard scopes; Junior designers develop under closer review. The Creative Director (Allie) continues to own craft quality and standards across all three tiers.

Development Team — organized by platform

Two platform-specific pods — WordPress and Shopify — each with its own technical standards and review process, rather than one generalist pool. A Development Lead owns technical quality and architecture calls across both platforms and staffs projects to the pod with the right expertise.

Marketing Team — organized by service

Two sub-teams: Content Production (organic content, editorial, creative assets) and Paid Content (paid media, ad campaigns, performance).

Continuity

How this maps to what we had

The rename is mostly a sharpening, not a reinvention. The Front-of-House / Back-of-House instinct from the Operating Framework is intact — it's now named for the outcomes each side owns. The one genuinely new thing is the Client Manager as a single, named revenue owner per account, which pulls sales ownership out of the Consultant seat.

Before Rollout

Reconciliation & open questions

Camila's chart is strong and the direction is settled. What's genuinely open are a handful of named-assignment and sequencing questions — some Camila flagged directly, some that fall out of the rename when set against the prior model. None of these are blockers; they're the decisions to make before the terminology goes live to the team.

Does the Coordinator stay split by track?

Lucy (Talent) and Andrea (Trades) were a settled by-track concierge split. The chart renames the role to Client Coordinator and doesn't carry the split. Deliberate consolidation, or should the track split survive under the new name? The client-experience economics of Talent vs. Trades are different enough that this is worth a decision, not a default.

Flag · reconciliation

Integrator / COO placement

The chart shows the two teams but not the seat above them. Camila functions as COO / Integrator over both — worth drawing explicitly, since the whole two-team accountability model depends on someone owning the seam between revenue and production. (Consent to the formal Integrator role is still gated on the July financial infrastructure landing.)

Matt appears as a tier-one Client Consultant (technical builds). The Development Lead who owns architecture across the WordPress/Shopify pods is described but not named. If that's still Matt, he's on both teams — worth naming intentionally, or handing the Dev Lead seat to someone else so the Consultant role is clean.
