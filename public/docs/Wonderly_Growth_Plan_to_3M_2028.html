The Goal

Triple the business in three years — from $1.12M to $3.0M — without an acquisition, a bet-the-company anchor, or a year of chaos. This is a growth plan you can run on rhythm.

The shape of the plan: revenue grows ~37–40% a year, every year, through four organic levers — pricing, a higher-value client mix, a recurring-revenue engine, and selective net-new sales — spread across the Talent and Trades tracks. No single deal has to land for the year to work, which is what makes a three-year horizon achievable rather than heroic.

Why the extra year matters: a 2027 sprint to $3M would have required nearly doubling in a single year and a forced inorganic move. Stretching to 2028 turns that into a steady organic climb, gives gross margin three years to rise from 50% to 58%, and lets overhead fall as a share of revenue gradually instead of all at once. Growth and margin compound together rather than fighting each other.

The plan keeps a firm profit discipline underneath the growth — a 25% net target, distributed cleanly to owners and shared with the team — but that's the engine room, detailed in Section 09. The story up front is the growth itself: where it comes from, how it's sold, and what it takes to deliver it.

The Growth Levers

Four levers, ordered by confidence and control. The first two you pull now; the second two you build through 2026–27 into the engine that carries the back half of the plan.

Lever 01 · High confidence
Pricing & rate realization
2028 Add+$300K

The fastest growth on the board: revenue with no new delivery cost. Your blended effective rate of ~$85/hr sits well under the $120 your quality supports, and not a single retainer escalates today.

  • Move the blended effective rate to $110–120/hr in 2026; path to $135 by 2028.
  • Install a $15K project minimum, enforced at the strategy-kickoff gate.
  • Add a 5–8% annual escalator to every retainer at renewal.
  • Reprice the known eroders now (Pasta Queen at 29% margin, the AYOH overrun). Each unrepriced month costs ~$3–4K.
Lever 02 · High confidence
Higher-value client mix
2028 Add+$250K

153 clients at $7,206 becomes ~90 at $33K. The growth engine here is replacement: graduate the smallest, most time-hungry accounts and put a $30K+ engagement in each freed slot. Top-5 concentration is a healthy 34% (largest client ~10%), so accounts can grow without dependency risk.

  • Rank the book by margin (Harvest hours ÷ revenue); flag the bottom ~30 time-sinks.
  • Reprice, restructure, or graduate them — replace each with a larger, better-fit client.
  • Hold a hard floor: no new client below the project minimum.
  • Track revenue-per-active-client monthly — the single clearest proxy for the strategy working.
Lever 03 · Build through 2026–27
A recurring-revenue engine
2028 Add+$450K

The lever that makes the growth durable. Recurring revenue compounds, scales faster than project headcount, and is the truest form of your "owned infrastructure outlasts rented attention" thesis. Target ≥30% of revenue recurring (~$75K MRR) by 2028.

  • Productize care plans, hosting, security/maintenance, and analytics into fixed monthly tiers.
  • Launch Trades-Track "growth retainers" — ongoing dev + marketing subscriptions, not one-off builds.
  • Convert one-and-done website clients into managed relationships at handoff — renewal designed in, not chased.
  • Make recurring the base load the rest of the plan grows on top of.
Lever 04 · Ramp the engine
Net-new sales, both tracks
2028 Add+$650K

The largest single contributor, and the one that demands a real demand engine. Pipeline at 2× the monthly target on each track, fed by the four-pillar content system — owned platforms first — so you're choosing the best-fit, highest-margin work rather than taking whatever comes.

  • Hold pipeline ≥ 2× monthly revenue target on each track, reviewed weekly in L10.
  • Run the four-pillar content engine as the primary lead source.
  • Use the Senior Developer hire to unlock premium Trades builds you can credibly bid.
  • Formalize the Maria ↔ Tori intake path so demand converts to briefed work without a founder bottleneck.

The Trades Track is the structural unlock for scale: it's a second revenue engine sharing the same back office, discipline leads, and tools — so growing it adds revenue without duplicating overhead. The leverage: shared infrastructure means the second $1.2M of Trades revenue costs far less overhead than the first $1.2M of Talent revenue did. That's where the operating leverage in this plan comes from — growth that reuses what you've already built.

05

Growth That Stays Profitable

Tripling revenue is only worth it if the margin comes with it. Four disciplines keep net margin climbing to 25% as the top line grows.

The margin bridge

Driver20252028Mechanism
Gross margin50%58%Rate up 40%, utilization 60%→68%, brief discipline, recurring mix
OpEx as % of revenue41%33%Shared infrastructure — overhead grows slower than revenue
Net margin8.6%25.0%$750K net on $3.0M

Pricing and utilization drive the gross line: the same 40% rate increase that grows revenue lifts margin, and blended utilization rising from 60% to 68% means each person produces more billable value without working more hours.

Brief discipline is the load-bearing wall. The strategy-kickoff gate, scope/out-of-scope definition, and change orders are what stop growth from eroding margin. The AYOH overrun (93% of budget gone before build) and the Pasta Queen 2% month are what a discretionary gate costs. As volume rises, the gate stays structural in ClickUp — no exceptions.

Operating leverage does the rest: leadership comp, the tool stack, and admin are largely semi-fixed, so spread across nearly triple the revenue they fall from 41% to 33% of the top line even as their dollar value rises.

Founders out of delivery — Adam's delivery utilization from 47% to ≤25%, Allie's below 30% — moves your most expensive capacity from billable work to the strategy and sales that actually build the growth.

Risks & the Honest Read

A plan you can trust names its weak points. These are the four most likely to slow the climb to $3M.

Net-new sales under-deliver
Highest risk
Lever 04 is the biggest contributor (+$650K) and the least certain — it needs a demand engine that doesn't fully exist yet. Mitigation: stand up the content engine and the Coordinator hire early; hold pipeline ≥ 2× target; lean harder on the higher-confidence pricing and recurring levers if sales lag.
Hiring ahead of revenue inverts margin
Medium
Growth tempts you to staff ahead of the work. Mitigation: trigger-before-hire, weekly utilization review; only the Senior Developer leads its trigger, and only because it's a revenue enabler.
Brief discipline slips under volume
Medium
More work means more pressure to skip the gate — and the AYOH/Pasta Queen patterns are what that costs. Mitigation: the strategy-kickoff gate stays structural in ClickUp; scope expansion without it is a process violation, not a judgment call.
Cash constrains the growth
Medium
$70K cash and $84K overdue leave little room to fund hiring and demand-gen. Mitigation: the Section 06 sequence — collect, clear the card, build a 3-month reserve — before leaning into spend.

The honest bottom line: $3M by 2028 is an ambitious but achievable organic climb — ~39% a year, no acquisition required, every lever traceable to something you already do. The single biggest dependency is building a real net-new sales engine; everything else is within your control today. Hold the margin discipline underneath it and the growth is worth having, not just impressive on a slide.
